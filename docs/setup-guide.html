Job Matcher

Set up and send your first application

Eight steps, about fifteen minutes. At the end you'll have a daily job digest arriving by email and your first application sent — with your resume attached, from your own address.

Before you start

  • 1The site address and an invite code. Both come from whoever runs this instance. You can't register without the code.
  • 2A Gmail account with 2-Step Verification on. Any mail provider works, but Gmail is the path of least resistance and 2FA is required for what comes next.
  • 3Your resume as a PDF, under 5 MB.
1

Create your account

Go to /signup on the site address you were given. Enter your email, a password of at least 8 characters, and the invite code.

You'll land straight on the Mail setup page, because nothing can be sent until that's done.

2

Create a Gmail app password

Job Matcher sends mail as you, so it needs its own password for your mail account — not your real one.

With 2-Step Verification switched on, visit Google App Passwords, create one, and copy the 16 characters.

Your normal Google password will not work here. If App Passwords isn't offered as an option, 2-Step Verification isn't fully enabled yet — turn it on and come back.
3

Connect your mail account

On Mail setup, fill in these values:

SMTP host
smtp.gmail.com
Port
587
Implicit TLS
leave unticked
SMTP username
you@gmail.com
App password
the 16 characters, no spaces
Send from
you@gmail.com
Send digests to
you@gmail.com

Google shows the app password in four groups of four. Type it as one unbroken string.

Send from must match the SMTP username exactly. Gmail refuses to send mail claiming to be from an address you haven't authenticated as.
Your app password is encrypted before it's stored, and the page will never show it back to you. To change it later, type a new one; leave the field blank to keep the existing one.
4

Send a test email

Once the settings are saved, a Send test email button appears. Press it and check your inbox — and your spam folder.

Do this now rather than later. If something is wrong with your credentials, you want to find out here, not halfway through applying for a job.

5

Fill in your profile

The Profile page has two halves. The first decides which jobs get found:

  • Desired job titles — what you'd type into a job site. Each title costs one API request per search, so start with one or two.
  • Skills — these drive your match score, so list what you'd genuinely claim in an interview.
  • Preferred locations, exclude keywords, employment types, minimum score, and how far back to search.

The second half — name, headline, pitch, links — is what gets written into your application drafts, word for word.

Only your first location is actually searched. The rest still affect your match score, but they don't widen the net. If you want results from a second city, that's a second search.
6

Upload your resume

At the bottom of the Profile page, upload your resume once — PDF or Word, up to 5 MB. Every application draft from then on offers to attach it with a single tick, already ticked.

7

Run your first search

Press Run search in the top bar. It runs immediately instead of waiting for tomorrow's scheduled run, then drops you on the Drafts page with a summary of what it found.

Nothing found? Widen Posted within to month, lower your minimum score, or use a broader job title. A quiet result usually means the filters are tight, not that the market is empty.

8

Send an application

Each match arrives as a prepared draft. Click one to open it, then:

  • Put the recruiter's address in To. You have to find this yourself — job listings almost never include it, so check the posting or the company's site.
  • Edit the subject and body. They're a starting point, not a finished letter.
  • Confirm your resume is ticked under Attachments, and add a cover letter there if you have one.
  • Press Send application.
Nothing reaches a recruiter automatically. The daily run only ever emails you. A draft sits untouched until you personally press Send on it.

How your match score works

Every job is scored out of 100. Anything at or above your minimum score becomes a draft.

SignalPoints
Job title contains one of your desired titles40
Your skills appear in the title or descriptionup to 40
Location matches, or the job is remote20
An excluded keyword appears anywherescore → 0

Because location is only worth 20, a strong title-and-skills match in the wrong city still scores 80 and gets through. If you want location to be close to mandatory, set your minimum score above 80.

Worth knowing

Daily digest
Runs once a day at 07:00 UTC and emails you only if something new turned up.
Repeat matches
A job you've already been told about won't be sent again.
Draft lifespan
Drafts are kept for 30 days, then cleared automatically.
Attachment limit
5 MB for your stored resume; 10 MB total per email.
Drafts are templates
Nothing is AI-written. Every sentence comes from text you wrote in your profile, so the email can't invent experience you don't have.
Your data
Your profile, drafts and resume are yours alone — no other user can see them.

If something goes wrong

“Username and Password not accepted”

The app password is wrong, or 2-Step Verification isn't actually switched on. Generate a fresh app password and retype it without spaces.

The test email never arrives

Check spam first. If it's genuinely missing, confirm Send from is identical to your SMTP username — a mismatch is the usual cause.

The search finds nothing

Widen Posted within to month, drop your minimum score, or try a broader job title. Recent postings for a narrow title in one city can genuinely be zero.

A send fails partway through

Your edits are saved before delivery is attempted, so nothing you typed is lost. Fix the problem and press Send again.

“Set up your mail account first”

You haven't finished Mail setup, or the saved credentials were removed. Go back to step 3.

Job Matcher runs on your own invite-only instance. Applications are sent from your mail account, never a shared one.
